ABSTRACT

The "Virtual Reality-Based Virtual Museum Experience" is an interactive, browser-based three-dimensional digital heritage and educational environment developed for the course Foundations of Virtual Reality. Traditional physical museums are constrained by geographical distance, admission fees, opening hours, physical accessibility limitations, and security glass barriers that prevent close-range interactive observation. This project eliminates those constraints by constructing a digital museum complex accessible through standard modern web browsers without requiring proprietary downloads, plugins, or external asset dependencies.

The application is engineered using HTML5, CSS3, modular JavaScript (ES6), and Three.js (r158). The virtual museum features six interconnected spatial wings: a Grand Main Lobby, a Central Atrium Hub, an Art Gallery (West Wing), a Historical Artifacts Gallery (East Wing), a Science and Technology Wing (South-West), and a Central Exhibition Hall (South-East). Users navigate the museum using a first-person 360-degree camera driven by PointerLockControls, keyboard locomotion (WASD with sprint capabilities), real-time Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) collision boundary handling, and a dynamic heads-up display (HUD) equipped with an interactive crosshair and real-time mini-map radar.

Interactive inspection is driven by screen-center Raycaster mathematics that dynamically highlight 3D artifacts and historical paintings with emissive glows and trigger informational modals containing historical context, technical notes, and key facts. Audio immersion is synthesized entirely in real time using the Web Audio API, delivering procedural ambient room acoustics, physical footstep sounds, and modal chimes. The project is mapped directly to United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4 – Quality Education), proving that spatial 3D and Virtual Reality principles can democratize equitable, immersive, and zero-cost cultural and scientific education for students worldwide.

Keywords: Virtual Reality, Three.js, WebGL, 3D Interactive Environment, PointerLockControls, Raycasting, Procedural Textures, Web Audio API, SDG 4 (Quality Education).

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background of Virtual Reality

Virtual Reality (VR) is a computer-generated simulation of a three-dimensional environment with which a person can interact in a seemingly real or physical manner. Unlike traditional flat screen interfaces that present information passively on a two-dimensional surface, VR technology immerses the user directly inside the digital environment. In VR systems, human sensory channels—predominantly vision and audition—are coupled dynamically to computer-rendered spatial representations. By recalculating the visual projection in real time as the user tilts, rotates, or translates their point of view, the system synthesizes visual immersion and spatial presence.

In modern engineering practice, Virtual Reality has evolved from specialized, cost-prohibitive head-mounted displays into standard web technologies through the Khronos Group's WebGL (Web Graphics Library) and WebXR Device API specifications. WebGL enables low-level direct access to the graphics processing unit (GPU) from standard web browsers via the HTML5 canvas element, eliminating third-party binary plugins. High-level JavaScript graphics abstractions, notably Three.js, have made it feasible to design complex architectural spaces, multi-source illumination models, and interactive kinematics that run efficiently on standard consumer hardware.

1.2 Concept and Significance of Virtual Museums

A Virtual Museum is an interactive digital entity that mirrors or augments the characteristics of a physical museum to provide access to historical, scientific, and cultural knowledge. While physical museums serve as vital centers of civilization, they face fundamental limitations. Physical collections are static, geographical travel is expensive, vulnerable historical artifacts degrade under atmospheric exposure, and crowded galleries restrict sustained observation.

A 3D virtual museum overcomes these barriers by transforming education from passive rote memorization into active, self-paced spatial exploration. In an immersive virtual museum, visitors can approach exhibits at close range, inspect artifacts from any angle, trigger interactive multi-tier educational descriptions, and transition seamlessly across different chronological eras and scientific domains—such as moving from the Italian High Renaissance directly into quantum physics and astronomical scale models.

1.3 Purpose of the Project

This microproject, completed for the course Foundations of Virtual Reality, designs, implements, and evaluates a fully functional, self-contained 3D Virtual Museum. The application is built from first principles using HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript (ES6 Modules), and Three.js. The system demonstrates the practical application of 3D transformation matrices, perspective projection, scene graph management, raycasting collision and selection mathematics, and procedural Web Audio synthesis within an engineering-grade educational framework.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Access to high-quality cultural, artistic, and scientific museum exhibitions is inequitably distributed across the globe. Traditional physical museums require physical attendance, which imposes substantial economic costs, travel time, and logistical hurdles for students, particularly those in rural or underfunded educational institutions. Furthermore, physical museums are constrained by limited floor space, fixed operating hours, restrictive glass barriers around fragile artifacts, and lack of interactive multimedial feedback.

Conversely, conventional museum websites typically offer only two-dimensional, static photographic catalogues or non-interactive 360-degree panoramic photos (such as Google Street View imagery), which lack genuine depth perception, physical locomotion, spatial presence, and engaging interactive feedback.

Therefore, there is an acute educational need for a lightweight, accessible, zero-cost, and fully interactive 3D Virtual Museum environment that operates directly within standard modern web browsers. Such an application must provide realistic first-person navigation, responsive collision boundaries, spatial exhibit inspection, rich educational contextualization, and integrated audio feedback without requiring high-end graphics hardware, paid software licenses, or complex installations.

4. EXISTING SYSTEM VS. PROPOSED SYSTEM

4.1 Analysis of the Existing System

Existing approaches to museum dissemination generally fall into two categories:

  • Physical Museums: Offer irreplaceable physical authenticity but are geographically immobile, subject to entrance fees, closed outside scheduled hours, and often overcrowded. Visitors cannot touch exhibits or inspect artifacts closely due to velvet stanchions and protective vitrines.
  • Traditional Museum Websites & Digital Catalogues: Present exhibits as 2D digital photographs alongside static blocks of HTML text. While accessible over the internet, they lack spatial dimensionality, depth cues, and exploration agency. The user acts as an observer flipping through an online catalogue rather than an active explorer discovering an exhibit space.
  • Panoramic 360-Degree Virtual Tours: While providing photographic fidelity, pre-rendered 360-degree tours (such as Matterport scans) restrict the user to discrete "teleportation" jumps between fixed nodes. Users cannot move freely, inspect 3D geometry from custom vantage points, or experience real-time dynamic lighting and orbital physics.

5. TECHNOLOGIES USED

The project is developed using modern, standards-compliant web technologies without external framework overhead:

  • HTML5 (HyperText Markup Language 5): Provides the semantic backbone of the application. It hosts the <canvas> rendering context for Three.js, structures the Heads-Up Display (HUD), mini-map, modal dialogue boxes, help screens, and loading overlay. Additionally, hidden HTML5 2D canvas elements are utilized programmatically as procedural texture rendering surfaces.
  • CSS3 (Cascading Style Sheets 3): Formats the user interface with an elegant contemporary museum visual aesthetic. Utilizes modern CSS variables, CSS grid and flexbox models, backdrop-filter blur effects for semi-transparent frosted HUD panels, custom keyframe animations (such as floating loading icons), and responsive modal styling.
  • JavaScript (ECMAScript 6+ / ES Modules): Powers all client-side computational logic, split cleanly across modular scripts:
    • main.js: Master orchestrator, animation render loop, state management, and HUD mini-map canvas renderer.
    • museum.js: Architectural synthesis, wall geometry, floor zoning, lighting configuration, colonnades, and procedural texture generators.
    • controls.js: Event listener bindings, first-person kinematic calculations, and Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) collision checks.
    • exhibits.js: 3D exhibit modeling, procedural artwork canvas painting, pedestal generation, and comprehensive educational catalogue data.
    • interaction.js: Three.js Raycaster target evaluation, emissive highlight shader modification, dynamic crosshairs, and modal display handling.
    • audio.js: Synthesized audio engine using the browser's native Web Audio API (oscillators, biquad filters, and noise buffers).
  • Three.js (Release 158): The industry-standard JavaScript 3D engine. Utilized for scene graph management, PerspectiveCamera projection, WebGLRenderer rasterization, PCF soft shadow mapping, ACES Filmic tone mapping, standard PBR materials (MeshStandardMaterial), and PointerLockControls.
  • WebGL (Web Graphics Library): The underlying low-level browser API that executes hardware-accelerated vertex and fragment shaders on the visitor's local GPU.
  • WebXR Device API: Included within main.js as an optional hardware abstraction layer that probes for connected immersive VR headsets (e.g., Oculus/Meta Quest or HTC Vive) via navigator.xr.isSessionSupported('immersive-vr').

8. MODULE DESCRIPTION

The software architecture is partitioned into six decoupled functional modules, each fulfilling a specific role in the graphics, interaction, or simulation pipeline:

A. Museum Architectural Environment Module (museum.js)

Purpose: Constructs the complete physical layout of the museum, covering floor surfaces, perimeter and partition walls, architectural archways, fluted columns, benches, velvet stanchion barriers, and lighting.

Working: Rather than loading megabytes of static 3D files over the network, this module procedurally synthesizes custom HTML5 canvas textures at runtime:

  • createMarbleTexture(): Synthesizes light and dark polished Italian marble with natural random veining and gold grout lines.
  • createParquetTexture(): Generates multi-tone herringbone hardwood planks for the classical Art Gallery.
  • createStoneTexture(): Simulates ancient porous travertine stone tiles for the Historical Artifacts Gallery.
  • createTechFloorTexture(): Generates a futuristic navy grid with cyan glowing circuit seam lines for the Science Wing.

The module also establishes a realistic multi-light illumination hierarchy: a warm ambient fill light, a hemisphere sky-ground bounce light, a directional skylight casting PCF soft shadows, and zone-specific point and spotlights tailored to each gallery's mood.

B. Locomotion & Collision Module (controls.js)

Purpose: Handles user locomotion, sprint acceleration, mouse looking, camera altitude stabilization, and spatial boundary constraint.

Working: Implements PointerLockControls to capture mouse movement without cursor drift. When movement keys (W, A, S, D or Arrow keys) are depressed, the module projects the camera's directional look vector onto the horizontal XZ plane. The proposed displacement step is tested against an array of 15 overlapping Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABB) representing the walkable floor space: 

// Independent X and Z collision tests enable smooth sliding along gallery walls:
if (isPositionWalkable(nextX, camera.position.z)) {
  camera.position.x = nextX;
}
if (isPositionWalkable(camera.position.x, nextZ)) {
  camera.position.z = nextZ;
}

Camera altitude is locked at 1.7 meters (human eye height). Footstep audio triggers automatically when valid displacement occurs.

C. Exhibition Catalogue & 3D Modeling Module (exhibits.js)

Purpose: Houses the 3D mathematical representations of the 15 exhibits, builds custom gilded frames and museum pedestals, and registers interactive metadata.

Working: Generates artworks and artifacts procedurally using Three.js primitive geometries (spheres, cylinders, toruses, boxes, cones) combined with 2D canvas texture generation for paintings. The catalogue spans four major exhibition domains:

  • Art Gallery (5 Exhibits): The Starry Night (Van Gogh), Mona Lisa (Leonardo da Vinci), The Great Wave off Kanagawa (Hokusai), Girl with a Pearl Earring (Vermeer), and Composition with Red, Blue, and Yellow (Mondrian). Each is mounted in a gilded frame with a dedicated brass picture lamp, wall spotlight, and inscribed brass plaque.
  • Historical Artifacts (4 Exhibits): Tutankhamun's Golden Sarcophagus with pharaonic crook and flail inside a glass vitrine, The Rosetta Stone with three distinct scripts, Roman Imperial Bust of Augustus in Carrara marble, and a Norse Viking Bearded Axe with round shield.
  • Science & Technology (3 Exhibits): The Bohr Quantum Atom with rotating electron rings and orbiting electron spheres, the DNA Double Helix with 64 nitrogenous base rungs, and a modern Virtual Reality Headset with dual optical lenses and spatial audio straps.
  • Central Hall & Atrium (3 Exhibits): The Planet Earth Globe with procedural continents, swirling cloud layers, and an orbiting solar-powered satellite; the Solar System Orrery with the Sun and 6 rotating planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn with rings); and the Great Pyramid of Giza with 5 stepped tiers and a golden pyramidion capstone.

D. Interaction & Raycasting Module (interaction.js)

Purpose: Performs continuous screen-center target intersection, visually alerts the user when an exhibit is within range, and manages informational modal dialogues.

Working: On every animation frame, a THREE.Raycaster is cast through normalized device coordinates (0, 0), corresponding to the center crosshair. If an exhibit mesh is intersected within an interactive radius of 6.8 meters:

  1. The center crosshair transitions from neutral white to an active golden target ring.
  2. A bottom HUD toast appears displaying: "[Exhibit Title] — Press E or Click to Explore".
  3. The exhibit's material dynamically increases its emissive color intensity (or scales up by 5%), providing immediate visual feedback.

When the user presses E or clicks the left mouse button, pointer lock is cleanly released, a two-tone chime sound plays, and a comprehensive modal panel displays the exhibit's category, historical context, technical background, takeaway fact, and SDG 4 connection.

E. Procedural Web Audio Engine (audio.js)

Purpose: Delivers spatial and interaction audio feedback without external audio files, ensuring instant loading and complete cross-origin stability.

Working: Uses the native browser AudioContext:

  • Ambient Room Drone: Synthesizes a subtle, warm gallery resonance using a 55 Hz sine-wave oscillator paired with a 110 Hz triangle oscillator routed through a 220 Hz lowpass biquad filter.
  • Footstep Synthesis: Generates a 60ms white-noise buffer passed through an 800–1000 Hz lowpass filter with an exponential gain decay to simulate leather soles striking polished marble floor tiles.
  • UI Chimes: Generates pleasant arpeggiated frequency sweeps (E5 to B5 for opening, D5 to A4 for closing) using sine oscillators.

F. Master Orchestration & Mini-Map Module (main.js)

Purpose: Serves as the central application entry point, controls the loading sequence, drives the 60 FPS requestAnimationFrame loop, tracks gallery zone transitions, and renders the real-time mini-map radar.

Working: Manages application startup, resizes the camera projection matrix upon browser window adjustments, tracks visitor coordinates to pop up gallery transition toasts (e.g., "Entering Art Gallery"), and dynamically draws a 2D architectural floor plan onto the HUD mini-map canvas, rendering the visitor's real-time position beacon and directional vision cone.

9.2 Procedural Canvas Texture Generation

To ensure that the application functions offline without missing asset errors or CORS security blocks, textures are synthesized dynamically using HTML5 Canvas 2D contexts: 

// Procedural Italian Marble Generator (js/museum.js)
function createMarbleTexture(isDark = false) {
  const canvas = document.createElement('canvas');
  canvas.width = 1024; canvas.height = 1024;
  const ctx = canvas.getContext('2d');
  ctx.fillStyle = isDark ? '#14121a' : '#eae5d8';
  ctx.fillRect(0, 0, 1024, 1024);

  // Random veining simulation
  ctx.strokeStyle = isDark ? 'rgba(80, 70, 95, 0.25)' : 'rgba(180, 170, 155, 0.35)';
  ctx.lineWidth = 3;
  for (let i = 0; i < 24; i++) {
    ctx.beginPath();
    let x = Math.random() * 1024, y = Math.random() * 1024;
    ctx.moveTo(x, y);
    for (let j = 0; j < 6; j++) {
      x += (Math.random() - 0.5) * 220;
      y += (Math.random() - 0.3) * 220;
      ctx.lineTo(x, y);
    }
    ctx.stroke();
  }
  const tex = new THREE.CanvasTexture(canvas);
  tex.wrapS = tex.wrapT = THREE.RepeatWrapping;
  return tex;
}

9.3 Real-Time Orbital Kinematics

The Science Wing and Central Hall feature continuous multi-body animations calculated each frame using delta time elapsed from THREE.Clock: 

// Multi-axis Orbit kinematics for Bohr Atom Electrons and Planets (js/interaction.js)
function updateOrbits(scene) {
  const elapsed = orbitClock.getElapsedTime();
  scene.traverse((obj) => {
    if (obj.userData.orbitInXZ && obj.userData.orbitCentre) {
      const c = obj.userData.orbitCentre;
      const ang = obj.userData.orbitAngle + elapsed * (obj.userData.orbitSpeed || 1.0);
      obj.position.set(c.x + Math.cos(ang) * obj.userData.orbitRadius, c.y, c.z + Math.sin(ang) * obj.userData.orbitRadius);
    }
  });
}

13. SDG MAPPING (SDG 4 – QUALITY EDUCATION)

UNITED NATIONS SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOAL 4: QUALITY EDUCATION

"Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all."

This microproject directly maps to and advances the core targets of UN SDG 4 through the following concrete mechanisms:

  • Democratizing Cultural and Scientific Access: Traditional visits to world-renowned cultural institutions—such as the Louvre in Paris, the British Museum in London, or the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York—are financially and geographically impossible for the vast majority of students globally. This virtual museum brings high-fidelity representations of world heritage directly into any classroom with an internet connection at zero financial cost.
  • Multidisciplinary STEAM Learning: The museum curates exhibits across distinct domains (visual art, linguistics, classical sculpture, metallurgy, quantum physics, molecular genetics, and space geodesy), encouraging holistic interdisciplinary thinking.
  • Spatial and Cognitive Retention: Educational psychology demonstrates that spatial exploration fosters superior memory retention compared to passive textbook reading. Navigating through a physical 3D layout to discover an exhibit grounds information in spatial memory.
  • Self-Paced, Inclusive Learning: Students can explore at their own speed without crowd pressure, re-reading historical context, inspecting artifacts from unique angles, and accessing key takeaways tailored for comprehension.
  • Zero Hardware Barrier: By engineering the project to operate smoothly in standard web browsers without requiring expensive VR headsets or high-end graphics cards, the project avoids the digital divide, making immersive education equitable.

14. ADVANTAGES AND LIMITATIONS

14.1 Advantages of the Project

  • Zero External Asset Vulnerability: Because textures, models, and audio are procedurally generated in the browser, the application never suffers from broken CDN links, CORS loading blocks, or 404 image errors.
  • Platform Independence: Functions across any desktop operating system (Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS) equipped with a standard modern web browser.
  • Lightweight Footprint: Loads instantaneously over modest network connections due to its ultra-compact code base (< 2 MB).
  • Smooth Kinematics & Physics: AABB collision detection ensures realistic spatial constraints with fluid wall-sliding, preventing unnatural boundary clipping.
  • Integrated Audio Immersion: Real-time synthesized ambient drone, footsteps, and interaction chimes provide auditory feedback without audio media dependencies.
  • Rich Curated Educational Context: Every exhibit includes detailed descriptions, historical notes, takeaway facts, and explicit SDG 4 connections.

14.2 Limitations of the Current Implementation

  • Simplified Procedural Geometries: Complex biological and archaeological artifacts (e.g., Tutankhamun's face or the Roman bust) are constructed from primitive geometric combinations rather than photogrammetric high-polygon 3D scans.
  • Absence of Multi-User Presence: The current system operates as a single-player local experience without networked avatars for collaborative teacher-guided tours.
  • Audio Synthesis Customization: While procedural audio ensures zero dependencies, it lacks spoken-voice audio narration for visually impaired visitors.
  • Touchscreen Mobile Controls: The current navigation relies on keyboard WASD and mouse pointer locking, requiring physical keyboards and mice.

15. FUTURE SCOPE

The modular architecture of this project provides a solid foundation for significant future enhancements:

  • Integration of Photogrammetric 3D Scans: Incorporating optimized GLTF/GLB 3D models captured via LiDAR or photogrammetry to display museum artifacts with millimeter-level historical fidelity.
  • WebSockets / WebRTC Multiplayer Guided Tours: Implementing a collaborative networking layer allowing teachers to lead groups of students through the virtual galleries with synchronized position avatars and spatial voice communication.
  • Multilingual Spoken Audio Guides: Integrating the browser's native Web Speech Synthesis API to provide spoken audio tours in multiple regional and international languages.
  • AI-Powered Virtual Docent: Incorporating an interactive conversational AI guide (driven by large language models) to answer student questions dynamically inside the 3D museum.
  • Mobile Virtual Joystick & Gyroscope Support: Developing on-screen touch joysticks and mobile device orientation sensor integration for smartphone users.
  • Expanded Wing Collections: Adding dedicated wings for Paleontology (dinosaur fossils), Climate Science, and Renewable Energy technologies.

16. CONCLUSION

The "Virtual Reality-Based Virtual Museum Experience" successfully demonstrates how modern web-based 3D graphics technologies can create an engaging, accessible, and pedagogically rich educational environment. Developed for the course Foundations of Virtual Reality, the microproject synthesizes computer graphics theory—including perspective camera transformations, scene graph hierarchy, multi-light illumination, procedural canvas texturing, axis-aligned bounding box collision kinematics, screen-center raycasting, and Web Audio API synthesis—into a cohesive, functional system.

By eliminating heavy external 3D asset downloads, proprietary plugins, and expensive hardware requirements, the project embodies the core ideals of United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4 – Quality Education): breaking down geographical, physical, and financial barriers to bring world-class cultural, artistic, and scientific exhibitions directly to students worldwide. The resulting application serves as a practical, submission-ready testament to the transformative potential of Virtual Reality in modern engineering and democratic education.
